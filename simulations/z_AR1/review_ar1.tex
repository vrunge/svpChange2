\section{Study summary}\label{study-summary}

The corrected study uses stationary Gaussian AR(1) noise with
\texttt{n\ =\ 600}, \texttt{rho\ =\ 0.8}, unit marginal variance, four
patterns, jumps from 1 to 5 in steps of 0.2, 100 replications, and a
five-observation matching tolerance. The final result contains 33600
method rows and the four methods PELT AR1 approximate, PELT inflated,
DeCAFS AR1, SVP AR1Focus / right / c = 1.850.

The mandatory endpoint \texttt{n} is removed before one-to-one metric
matching. The reported metrics are precision, recall, F1, correct-number
probability, signal MSE, fitted segment count, and localization error.

\section{Calibration}\label{calibration}

The selected DeCAFS multiplier is 7.75 and its calibration null F1 is
0.99. The selected approximate-Pelt multiplier is 0.8; the selected
AR1Focus multiplier is 1.85.

DeCAFS is the calibration target at corrected null F1 0.99. The
approximate Pelt and AR1Focus candidates are selected by closest
calibration null F1 to that DeCAFS target. The inflated PELT method
remains a fixed paper baseline. Calibration and validation use 1,000
independent null series each, with deterministic seeds and Wilson
intervals.

\section{PELT approximation and
AR1Focus}\label{pelt-approximation-and-ar1focus}

The approximate AR(1) PELT method applies the package PELT
implementation to \texttt{Z\_t\ =\ Y\_\{t+1\}\ -\ rho\ Y\_t}, then maps
an endpoint \texttt{j} in the transformed series to endpoint
\texttt{j+1} in the original series. This is an approximation because
the complete AR(1) likelihood includes a boundary correction that is not
retained by ordinary transformed-data least squares.

AR1Focus is called with \texttt{test\ =\ "AR1Focus"} and
\texttt{subtests\ =\ "right"}. No \texttt{both} or multiscale SVP call
is included in the study.
